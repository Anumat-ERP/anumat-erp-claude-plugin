# Rehearsal protocol

Rehearsal turns the script into something the team can do under pressure. The
goal is not a perfect run; it is finding every failure before the audience
does.

Score each run with
`${CLAUDE_PLUGIN_ROOT}/skills/demo-storytelling/templates/rehearsal-rubric.md`.

## The five kinds of run

Do them in order. Each has a different purpose.

| Run | Count | Purpose | How |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1. Table read** | 1 | Does the story work in words? | Speaker reads the Say column aloud, seated. Time it. Cut if over |
| **2. Click-through** | 2+ | Does the golden path work? | Driver clicks the Do column in silence. Note every slow or fragile step |
| **3. Full run** | 3+ | Do words and clicks fit together in time? | Standing, full voice, timekeeper, recorded on a phone |
| **4. Disaster run** | 1 | Can the team recover? | A teammate breaks something mid-run: kills Wi-Fi, closes a tab. The team follows the recovery ladder |
| **5. Dress rehearsal** | 1 | Is it ready? | Demo machine, demo network, demo room if possible, backup switch included, Q&A after |

Reset the data before every run.

## Timing

- Time every full run with a stopwatch; write the time in the rubric.
- Also time checkpoints: when you reach the wow moment, when you start the
  ask. Compare to the script.
- If three runs in a row come in over 90% of the slot, cut a step. Don't
  plan to talk faster.
- The last runs should vary by less than 10 seconds. If they don't, the
  script is still being improvised.

## Recording and feedback

- Record every full run on a phone, from where the judges will sit.
- Watch at least one recording with the sound off (body, hands, eye contact,
  where you stand) and one with eyes closed (pace, pauses, fillers,
  volume).
- Feedback format: **one thing that worked, one thing to change**. More than
  one change per run and none of them stick.
- The speaker does not give themselves feedback during the run; they finish
  and then watch.
- Count filler words per minute on one recording. Write the number down.

## Rehearsing specific parts

- **The first 30 seconds:** rehearse five times more than anything else.
- **Transitions:** every handover between speaker and driver has a cue line;
  rehearse the handovers alone.
- **The wow moment:** rehearse the setup, the silence, and the pause after.
- **The ask:** said to a real person, making eye contact, five times.
- **Q&A:** five minutes of hostile questions from a teammate, each answer
  timed to 20 seconds. See
  `${CLAUDE_PLUGIN_ROOT}/skills/demo-storytelling/reference/qa.md`.

## When to stop

- Stop changing the script at T-1 day (or a few hours before a hackathon
  final). Changes after that cause more mistakes than they fix.
- Stop rehearsing an hour before the slot. Do the warm-up instead.
- A good rehearsal the night before is enough. Don't do a fifth run at
  midnight; sleep is part of the preparation.
