# Warm-up routine

About 15 minutes, done in the hour before the slot, finishing no more than
10 minutes before you go on. Performers warm up; presenters should too. Run it
as a checklist with
`${CLAUDE_PLUGIN_ROOT}/skills/demo-storytelling/templates/warmup-checklist.md`,
and the tech part with
`${CLAUDE_PLUGIN_ROOT}/skills/demo-storytelling/templates/tech-check.md`.

Find somewhere semi-private: a corridor, a stairwell, a bathroom. The voice
exercises sound silly; that is fine.

## Order and timing

| Minutes | Block | Goal |
|---|---|---|
| 0 to 3 | Breathing | Lower heart rate, steady the voice |
| 3 to 6 | Body | Release tension in shoulders, jaw, hands; stand grounded |
| 6 to 11 | Voice | Wake the voice, loosen articulation, find projection |
| 11 to 15 | Mind | Rehearse the opening in your head, lock in the first two lines |

The tech warm-up is separate and done earlier (see below), so nothing
technical is left for the last 15 minutes.

## Breathing (3 minutes)

**Box breathing.** Breathe in through the nose for 4 counts, hold 4, out
through the mouth for 4, hold 4. Repeat four to six rounds. Used by athletes
and in military stress training; it slows breathing and gives the mind a
simple task.

**Physiological sigh.** Two inhales through the nose, the second one a short
top-up on top of the first, then one long, slow exhale through the mouth.
Repeat two or three times. A 2023 study (Balban et al., *Cell Reports
Medicine*) found five minutes a day of this "cyclic sighing" improved mood and
lowered breathing rate more than mindfulness meditation did. One or two sighs
right before walking on is the quickest tool you have.

## Body (3 minutes)

- **Shoulder rolls:** ten backward, ten forward, slow. Then lift the shoulders
  to the ears, hold two seconds, drop.
- **Neck:** ear to shoulder each side, slow, no circles.
- **Jaw and face:** open the mouth wide, then scrunch the face small; three
  times. Massage the jaw hinge.
- **Hands:** shake them out for ten seconds. Open and close fists.
- **Grounding:** feet hip-width, weight even, knees soft. Feel the floor.
- **Power stance:** stand tall, feet apart, hands on hips or arms up, for one
  to two minutes.

**About power posing, honestly.** The 2010 study (Carney, Cuddy and Yap) that
made power posing famous claimed it raised testosterone, lowered cortisol and
increased risk-taking. Larger replications did not find the hormonal or
behavioural effects (Ranehill et al., 2015), and the study's first author
later said she no longer believed the effect was real. What has held up more
consistently is a small effect on how powerful people **say they feel**. So:
do it if it helps you feel steady and take up space, but don't expect it to
change your body chemistry. Open posture on stage is still worth it for how
you look to the room.

## Voice (5 minutes)

- **Lip trills:** blow air through loosely closed lips so they flutter, like a
  motorboat, and add a sound sliding low to high and back. 30 seconds. It
  warms the voice without strain.
- **Humming:** hum on "mmm" with lips closed, feeling buzz in the lips and
  nose. Slide up and down your range. 30 seconds.
- **Sirens:** on "ng" (as in "sing"), slide from your lowest note to your
  highest and back. Three times.
- **Tongue twisters**, slow first, then at speaking pace, crisp consonants:
  - "Red leather, yellow leather."
  - "Unique New York, unique New York."
  - "The tip of the tongue, the teeth, the lips."
  - "Peter Piper picked a peck of pickled peppers."
- **Project a line to the back wall:** pick your opening line. Say it to a
  wall 5 to 10 metres away as if someone there must hear it, from the belly,
  without shouting. Three times, then once at normal volume.
- Sip room-temperature water. Avoid milk and fizzy drinks just before.

## Mind (4 minutes)

- **Visualise the first 30 seconds:** close your eyes and see it in order:
  walking up, where you stand, looking at one friendly face, saying the first
  line, the first click, the screen changing. Run it twice, in real time.
- **Memorise the first two lines** until you can say them while doing
  something else. Say them aloud three times.
- **Reappraise:** say "I'm excited" instead of "I'm calm". Arousal reframed as
  excitement helps more than trying to suppress it.
- **One intention:** pick one delivery goal only, such as "pause after the
  number". Not ten.
- Last thing before walking on: one physiological sigh.

## Tech warm-up (done 60 to 30 minutes before)

Run by the driver, with the tech-check template. Do it on the actual stage
hardware if the organisers allow a line check; otherwise on the same
adapters and network.

- **Power:** laptop charged to 100% and plugged in on stage if possible.
  Phone charged. Clicker batteries.
- **Adapters:** HDMI and USB-C adapters, plus a spare of each. Test the actual
  cable.
- **Display:** mirroring on (not extended) unless you need presenter notes;
  resolution matches the projector; nothing important in the corners the
  projector crops.
- **Network:** venue Wi-Fi joined and tested *and* a phone hotspot ready,
  with its password known. Know which demo steps need the network.
- **Do Not Disturb:** OS focus mode on; chat, mail, calendar and updates
  quit; phone on silent.
- **Browser:** zoom set (125 to 150%), tabs open in order, bookmarks bar
  hidden, no private tabs, logged in to every account the script uses.
- **Sessions:** logged-in sessions checked so they won't expire mid-demo.
- **Data reset:** run the reset and confirm the first screen of the script.
- **Backup video:** on the desktop, on a USB stick, and in the cloud, opened
  once to confirm it plays.
- **Golden path:** one full silent click-through, then reset again.
