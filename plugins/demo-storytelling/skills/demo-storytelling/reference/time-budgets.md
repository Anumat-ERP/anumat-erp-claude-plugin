# Time budgets

Time is the hardest constraint and the one teams ignore most. Write to the
clock, not to the content.

## Words per minute

- Conversational speech runs at about 140 to 160 words per minute. Nervous
  speakers go faster.
- Aim for **130 to 150 wpm** on stage: slow enough to be followed, with room
  for pauses.
- Clicks, page loads and pauses take 25 to 40% of a demo. So a live demo holds
  fewer words than a talk of the same length:

| Slot | Talk only | Live demo |
|---|---|---|
| 1 minute | 130 to 150 words | about 100 words |
| 3 minutes | 400 to 450 words | about 300 words |
| 5 minutes | 650 to 750 words | about 500 words |
| 10 minutes | 1,300 to 1,500 words | about 1,000 words |

Count the words in the Say column. If it is over, cut a step, not the pauses.

## Plan to 90%

Script to 90% of the slot. A 3-minute slot gets a 2:40 script. The spare 20
seconds absorb a slow page, a laugh, or a nervous start. Running long is
worse than running short: judges cut you off before the ask.

## 1-minute script

For lightning rounds, sprint reviews, or an opening teaser.

| Time | Beat |
|---|---|
| 0:00 | Hook: the persona and the pain in one or two sentences |
| 0:10 | One-sentence pitch |
| 0:15 | The one thing, shown live: the core action, start to result |
| 0:45 | One number or one proof |
| 0:52 | Ask |

One idea. One screen if possible.

## 3-minute script

The hackathon standard.

| Time | Beat | Share |
|---|---|---|
| 0:00 | Hook and pain: named persona, specific moment, stakes | 15% |
| 0:25 | One-sentence pitch; the turn to the product | 5% |
| 0:35 | Idea 1, shown: the first step of the golden path | 20% |
| 1:15 | Idea 2, shown: builds on step one | 20% |
| 1:55 | Idea 3, the wow moment | 15% |
| 2:25 | Proof: one number, one fact | 5% |
| 2:35 | Ask, then thank you | 5% |
| 2:45 | Spare | ~8% |

## 5-minute script

Customer demos, demo days, longer finals.

| Time | Beat |
|---|---|
| 0:00 | Hook and pain, with stakes |
| 0:40 | One-sentence pitch; how it works in one sentence |
| 1:00 | Idea 1, shown |
| 2:00 | Idea 2, shown |
| 3:00 | Idea 3, the wow moment |
| 3:45 | Proof: result, pilot, quote |
| 4:15 | What's next, in one sentence; why this team |
| 4:30 | Ask |
| 4:45 | Spare |

## 10-minute pitch structure

Investor pitches and longer finals. Slides frame the story; the demo sits in
the middle. Outline it with
`${CLAUDE_PLUGIN_ROOT}/skills/demo-storytelling/templates/pitch-outline.md`.

| Time | Section | Content |
|---|---|---|
| 0:00 | Hook | Persona, moment, stakes. No logo slide talk |
| 1:00 | Problem | Who hurts, how often, what it costs; evidence it's real |
| 2:00 | Solution | One-sentence pitch; how it works in three steps |
| 2:30 | Demo | 3-minute golden path, as above |
| 5:30 | Proof and traction | Users, pilots, numbers, quotes |
| 6:30 | Market | Who else has this problem; how many; how you reach them |
| 7:15 | Model | How it makes money or saves money |
| 7:45 | Competition | Honest alternatives, and why you win |
| 8:15 | Team | Why these people can build this |
| 8:45 | Ask | Amount or action, and what it unlocks |
| 9:15 | Close | Back to the persona; the one-sentence pitch again |
| 9:30 | Spare | |

## Marking time in the script

- Every row of the run-of-show has a start time. The timekeeper holds up
  fingers or cards at fixed points ("1 minute left", "30 seconds").
- Mark one **cut line**: a row the speaker drops if they are behind at a
  checkpoint. Write it in the script, so nobody decides under pressure.
