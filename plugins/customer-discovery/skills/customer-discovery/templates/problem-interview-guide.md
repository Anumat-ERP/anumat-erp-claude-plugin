# Problem interview guide

For learning whether a problem is real, frequent, and costly. No pitch, no
demo. Rules: `${CLAUDE_PLUGIN_ROOT}/skills/customer-discovery/reference/mom-test.md`.

Segment: ____  Hypotheses being tested (IDs): ____
Lead: ____  Note-taker: ____  Length: 25-30 minutes

## 1. Open (2 min)

- Thank them. Say who you are and that you are early and learning, not selling.
- "There are no wrong answers. Detail about what actually happened helps most."
- Ask consent: "Is it OK if [note-taker] takes notes / if we record? It stays
  within our team and we can delete it on request."

## 2. Their world (5 min)

- Tell me about your role. What does a normal week look like?
- Where does [problem area] fit into that?

## 3. The last time (10-15 min)

- Tell me about the last time [problem] happened. When was it?
- What were you trying to get done?
- What did you do? Then what happened?
- Who else was involved?
- How long did it take? What did it cost (money, hours, mistakes, customers)?
- How often does this happen? When was the time before that?

## 4. Workarounds and spend (5 min)

- What do you use today to deal with it? How did you end up with that?
- What do you pay for it, in money or staff time?
- Have you looked for something better? What did you find? Why not switch?
- What is the most annoying part of how you do it now?

## 5. Priorities (3 min)

- Where does this rank among the problems you're dealing with this quarter?
- If you had budget for one fix this year, what would it be?

## 6. Close with an ask (3 min)

Choose one ask in advance:
- "Who else deals with this? Could you introduce me to two people?"
- "Could we talk again with [colleague] next week?"
- "We're running a small pilot in [month]. Would you want to be considered?"

Thank them. Note what they committed to.

## Avoid

- "Would you use…?", "Would you pay…?", "Do you think it's a good idea?"
- Describing your solution before the last five minutes, or at all.
- Leading questions ("Isn't it frustrating when…?").
