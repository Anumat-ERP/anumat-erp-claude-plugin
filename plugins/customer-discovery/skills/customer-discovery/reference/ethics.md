# Ethics of customer discovery

The people who talk to you are giving time and trust. Discovery done
dishonestly also produces bad data: people who feel misled stop telling you
the truth.

## Be honest about your stage

- Say you are early when you are. "We are exploring this problem and have not
  built anything yet" is a fine opening.
- Do not imply a product exists, has customers, or has features it does not.
- In concierge and Wizard of Oz tests, do not make false claims about
  automation in contracts or sales material. If a customer asks how it works,
  tell them.
- Do not pose as a researcher, student, or journalist to get a meeting you
  would not otherwise get.

## No fake scarcity or pressure

- No invented deadlines, fake "only 3 spots left", or made-up waiting lists.
- Founding-customer discounts are fine when they are real and stated plainly.
- Deposits should be refundable, with clear terms, and refunds honoured
  promptly.

## Consent to record

- Ask every time before recording audio or video, and before taking a
  screenshot of anything they share.
- Say who will hear or see it and how long you will keep it.
- Accept a no without pushback; take notes instead.
- Do not quote someone publicly, or name their company, without their explicit
  permission.

## Data privacy for notes

- Store notes and recordings in one access-controlled place, not scattered
  across personal devices and chats.
- Use interview IDs in synthesis boards; keep the mapping from ID to name
  separate.
- Collect only what you need. Avoid recording sensitive personal data (health,
  finances, personal details of third parties) unless essential, and then
  protect it more strictly.
- Delete recordings on request and on a schedule you have stated.
- Follow the data-protection law that applies where you and the interviewee
  are (for example GDPR in the EU); when unsure, ask a professional.

## Respect their time

- Keep to the time you asked for.
- Follow up with thanks, and with anything you promised.
- If you decide to kill the idea, tell the people who committed time or money,
  and return any deposits.
