# Jobs-to-be-Done and the switch interview

Jobs-to-be-Done (JTBD) treats a purchase as hiring a product to make progress
in some situation. People do not want a drill; they want the shelf up before
the in-laws arrive. The ideas come from Clayton Christensen's work, and the
switch interview described here from Bob Moesta and Chris Spiek (see
*Demand-Side Sales 101*).

The switch interview is for people who **recently changed** how they deal with
the problem: bought a tool, hired someone, built a spreadsheet, or dropped a
product. The switch is a real past decision, so it resists the polite lies
that opinion questions invite.

## Four forces

Every switch is a balance of four forces:

| Force | Direction | Question to find it |
|---|---|---|
| **Push** of the current situation | toward change | What was going wrong? What was the last straw? |
| **Pull** of the new solution | toward change | What did you picture life would be like after? |
| **Anxiety** about the new solution | against change | What worried you about switching? What almost stopped you? |
| **Habit** of the present | against change | What did you like about the old way? What was hard to give up? |

A switch happens only when push plus pull beat anxiety plus habit. Products
often fail not because pull is weak but because anxiety and habit were ignored.
That tells you what to build *and* what to say in sales: reduce anxiety
(trials, migration help, guarantees) as much as you add pull.

## The timeline

Walk the person through the switch as a story, in order:

1. **First thought.** When did you first realise things needed to change?
2. **Passive looking.** Did you notice options without actively searching?
3. **Event / active looking.** What happened that made you start searching?
   Who else was involved?
4. **Deciding.** What did you compare? What was the trade-off? What almost made
   you stay put?
5. **Buying / starting.** What happened on the day? Who signed or paid?
6. **Using.** First days after. Did it do what you hoped? What surprised you?

Record dates and people at each step. Gaps in the story are where the insight
usually is.

## Interview tips

- Interview people within about three months of the switch, while details are
  fresh.
- Ask about context: where they were, what time, who was there. Context
  jogs real memory.
- Interview people who switched *away* from a solution like yours, not only
  toward it.
- Do not ask what they want. Ask what they did.

## Writing a job statement

After several switch interviews, write the job as:

> When [situation], I want to [motivation], so I can [expected outcome].

Example: "When a new branch opens, I want stock counts I can trust from day
one, so I can stop driving between locations to check shelves."

A job statement is still a hypothesis. Test it in the next round.
