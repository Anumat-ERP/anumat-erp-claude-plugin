# Ideal customer profile and early adopters

The ideal customer profile (ICP) says who you will talk to first and sell to
first. It should be narrow enough that you can list real names. "SMEs" is not a
segment. "Owner-run pharmacies with 1-3 locations that still reconcile stock on
paper" is.

## What goes in an ICP

**For a business buyer (firmographics):**
- industry and sub-type
- size: employees, locations, revenue band
- geography and language
- the tools and processes they use today
- who feels the pain, who pays, who signs

**For a consumer (demographics and context):**
- life situation, role, and routine where the problem appears
- what they already spend on the problem
- where they gather, online and in person

**For both:**
- **Trigger events**: the moment the problem becomes urgent. A new regulation,
  opening a second location, a key employee leaving, an audit, a bad month.
  People in the middle of a trigger event are the easiest to reach and the most
  honest about pain, because the pain is fresh.
- **Current workaround and spend**: what they do now and what it costs them in
  money, hours, and risk.

## Early adopters: the "hair on fire" segment

Steve Blank describes the ideal first customer as an **earlyvangelist**. In
plain terms, they:

1. **Have the problem.**
2. **Know they have it.**
3. **Are actively looking for a fix**, and have a timeline.
4. **Have already built a workaround**, a spreadsheet, a hired person, a
   patchwork of tools.
5. **Have or can get budget** for a better fix.

Each trait further down the list makes the person rarer and more valuable. A
person with traits 4 and 5 will often pay for an unfinished product because
the workaround is worse. Look for them first. Everyone else can wait until the
product is better.

A quick way to say it: their hair is on fire, and they have already tried to
put it out with a bucket.

## The anti-ICP

Write down who you will *not* target yet, and why. Common entries:

- too large: long procurement, many stakeholders, custom demands;
- too small: cannot pay, or the pain is too rare;
- happy with the current tool, and no trigger event in sight;
- needs a feature you cannot build this year (compliance, integration);
- the friendly contact who likes you but does not have the problem.

The anti-ICP protects the team from saying yes to a loud prospect who would pull
the product in the wrong direction.

## How to choose the first segment

Score candidate segments 1-5 on:

| Criterion | Question |
|---|---|
| Pain | How much does the problem hurt, and how often? |
| Spend | Do they already pay for a workaround? |
| Reach | Can we name 50 of them and get to them this month? |
| Trigger | Is there a recurring event that makes the pain urgent? |
| Fit | Can we deliver something useful to them within weeks? |

Start with the highest total, not the largest market. You can widen later; a
segment too wide produces interviews that never agree.
